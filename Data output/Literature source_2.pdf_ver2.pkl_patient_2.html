- Patient Information: The patient is an 80-year-old woman with generalized myasthenia gravis who presented with general weakness, binocular diplopia, and dyspnea that developed 10 days earlier. She was not on any immunosuppressive agents at the time of presentation.
- Suspected Drug Information: The suspected drug is intravenous immunoglobulin, prescribed at 400 mg/kg/day for five days for the management of complications associated with myasthenia gravis. The dose of prednisolone was also increased to 60 mg daily.
- Adverse Event:
- Sudden cardiac arrest: Two days after intravenous immunoglobulin infusion, the patient suffered a sudden cardiac arrest.
- Elevated D-dimer: Laboratory findings indicated an elevated D-dimer level of 19.97 ug/ml.
- Pulmonary thromboembolism: Although chest computed tomography was not performed, hemodynamic instability and D-dimer elevation suggested pulmonary thromboembolism.
- Hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy: Electroencephalography showed burst-suppression patterns, and brain magnetic resonance imaging findings were compatible with hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy.
- Semi-comatose mentality: Despite the administration of phenobarbital and valproic acid, her semi-comatose mentality did not improve.
- Treatment Process: The patient was initially prescribed prednisolone and pyridostigmine for myasthenia gravis. Following the adverse events, enoxaparin 40 mg was initiated due to the suspected pulmonary thromboembolism, and phenobarbital and valproic acid were prescribed for hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy.
- Final outcome for the patient: After extensive consultation with her family members, a decision was made to suspend the life-saving treatment regimen.
- Report and Overall Opinion: The case report paper provides insights into the occurrence of thromboembolic complications following intravenous immunoglobulin therapy in patients with immune-mediated neurological disorders, emphasizing the need for cautious monitoring in critically ill elderly patients with vascular risk factors.
